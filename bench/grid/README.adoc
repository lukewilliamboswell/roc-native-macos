= The grid stress test

How far the platform scales: an N×N grid of small fixed-size boxes, for N =
10, 32, 100, 316 and 1000 (100 to a million cells). The pointer entering a
cell turns it hot, and a one-shot timer for that cell turns it back 500 ms
later, so a moving pointer leaves a fading trail. Nothing ticks globally;
the live timers are the trail. A reactive UI draws nothing between events,
so this measures three things: how many elements the platform holds, what
one event costs at that size, and what many short-lived timers cost.

*In short:* once a batch that only repaints skips layout, a hover answers
in under 1 ms up to 2,500 cells and about 4 ms at 10,000, and the trail
behaves at every size. Since engine patch 0003 a hover's engine time is
about 0.1 ms at every size (0.3 ms in all at 10,000 cells). The ceiling is opening the window: 10,000 cells
take 13–30 s and over a GB, and 22,500 never opened (killed in Auto Layout
at 3.4 GB). The engine's quadratic mount and Auto Layout are the walls;
see <<where-it-breaks>>.

It is a harness, not an example. The gate's `bench` stage builds it and runs
`run.sh --smoke`: a 4×4 grid and a 12-move sweep, which fails unless the
cells were hovered and every trail timer has cooled. That keeps it from going
stale without putting any load on the gate. `--smoke` then checks that mount
and turns scale: it mounts N=20 and N=60 (400 and 3,600 cells) and sweeps
40 moves across each. It fails if the larger mount engine time is more
than 27 times the smaller (9 times the cells, so 3 times the linear
expectation; the smaller time is floored at 2 ms; a quadratic mount gives
about 87), or if the hover turns' engine p50 at N=60 is more than 4 times
N=20's (a turn should not grow with the cells; the smaller is floored at
0.1 ms, the host's resolution; before engine patch 0003 the ratio was
about 7 to 9, 0.1 against 0.7 to 0.9 ms, and it is now 1). Engine patches
0001 and 0002 (`patches/roc-signals`) made mount linear and 0003 made
turns independent of the grid, all by working around compiler behaviour,
and this keeps them from regressing silently. `NO_SCALING=1` skips both.

== Running it

[source,sh]
----
blueprint run build -- --platform   # the host, once
bench/grid/run.sh                # N = 10 32 100 316 1000
bench/grid/run.sh 50 70          # other sizes
bench/grid/run.sh --smoke        # what the gate runs
TIMEOUT=600 bench/grid/run.sh 100
----

Each N is a fresh process. N reaches the app as the local storage key
`grid-n`, passed as a launch argument (`-grid-n 316`, Cocoa's argument
domain, read through `ROC_STORAGE_SUITE`), so the mount itself builds the
grid and time to first display is honest. `ROC_BENCH=1` turns on the host's
timings and a scripted sweep (`ROC_SCRIPT`) drives the pointer: it crosses
the grid's diagonal in 2N moves (at least 200, at most 2000), one every
10 ms, through the same hit test the mouse uses, then waits for the trail
to cool and prints `stats`. A run still going after `TIMEOUT` seconds (120)
is killed.

`stats` prints, on stdout:

* `views`: every `NSView` in the window, and the peak RSS (`getrusage`);
  `timers-peak` is the most timers alive at once (the trail), and
  `timers-live` those alive now;
* `first-display`: from `main` to the window drawn, with the mount turn's
  engine, apply and layout times and the first forced display;
* per kind of operation (`mount`, `hover`: one pointer move's turns, `timer`:
  one timer's turn), how many and their p50/p95/max in ms for each phase:
  `engine` (the Roc call), `apply` (decoding the commands into views),
  `layout` (the host's `sync`: arranging stacks and fitting the window) and
  `display` (a forced `displayIfNeeded`).

The sweep prints how long its moves took against the plan: a lagging sweep
means turns took longer than the 10 ms between moves.

=== The live HUD

`LIVE=1 bench/grid/run.sh 100` opens the grid with no script: move the mouse
yourself. Under `ROC_BENCH=1` (and only then) the host draws a small panel
over the window's top-right corner, refreshed twice a second from a host
timer, never through the engine, so it does not perturb what it shows. It
sits in the window's frame view, outside the content's layout. Over the
last second:

* *event→paint*, p50 and p95: from the mouse event's timestamp to the end
  of the forced display that drew its answer, which is what a person feels
  (the window server then composites it on the next refresh, about one more
  frame, which the host cannot see without linking QuartzCore);
* *draws/s*: operations that had something to draw. A reactive UI draws
  nothing when nothing changes, so a still pointer shows `idle`, not a
  frame rate; this counts the host's draws, not the display's refreshes;
* *moves/s* and how many were *lagging* (waited more than a 60 Hz frame for
  the main thread; AppKit itself coalesces moves before the host sees
  them), and engine *turns/s*;
* p95 per operation of engine, apply, layout and display time;
* live timers (the trail), the view count and the hover targets (recounted
  after a layout), the current RSS (`task_info`), and the time to first
  display.

The grid size is fixed per launch: relaunch at another N rather than switch
in the app. A switch would be cheap to write (`Engine.switch` over the
sizes) but measures badly: the engine evaluates every branch's description
at mount (1.2 s for the million-cell one), and remounting at 10,000 cells
or more takes as long as the first mount (the quadratic copy below).

The HUD's event→paint latency during the scripted sweep, after the fix:
p50 0.5 ms and p95 0.5 ms at 1,024 cells, p50 3.9 ms and p95 5.5 ms at

A hover move is the leave and enter turns of one pointer move (two engine
turns, one layout); a timer turn, cooling one cell, costs about half a
move's engine time (1.4 ms at 10,000 cells). The trail behaves: the live
timers peak at the trail's length (3 at N=10, 8 at N=32, 21 at N=100, for
500 ms of a 2 s sweep), never at N², and fall to 0 once it cools; every
cell entered was cooled by its own timer, and re-entering a hot cell
cancels its earlier timer (`watch_latest`).

== Where it breaks, and why

There are three costs, and each has its own cliff.

Layout on every turn (fixed):: Before 85a0951 every batch, even one that
only changed a cell's fill, ran the host's whole layout pass: it walked the
node tree, laid the window out twice and measured its fitting size.
`sample` of a 10,000-cell hover turn puts 99% of it in
`-[NSView systemLayoutSizeFittingSize:…]` →
`_populateEngineWithConstraintsForViewSubtree:forComputingFittingSizeOfView:`,
which builds a fresh Auto Layout solver over every view's constraints
(`NSLayoutConstraint _addToEngine:`, `expression_merge`, `NSBitSet…` in
CoreAutoLayout). That is 15 ms at 100 views, 52 ms at 1,000 and 2.1 s at
10,000: the platform stopped being interactive near 1,000 elements. A batch
that sets only `fill`, `border`, `radius` or `color` and changes no
structure or text now skips the pass and is only drawn. After it a hover
move is engine-bound at every size measured.

The engine's mount is quadratic (open):: Mounting static boxes alone takes
28 ms of engine time for 1,024 cells, 140 ms for 2,500, 1.2 s for 4,900 and
6.4 s for 10,000 (time grows as cells^2.4), with a peak RSS of 1.65 GB.
`sample` puts about 85% of it in `memcpy` under `list.RocList.reallocate`
(and most of the rest in freeing the old buffers: `free_medium`, `madvise`);
`lldb` on a `--debug` build places that `reallocate` in a `List.append`
inside `mount_elem` (Core.roc:5275, the recursive mount of an element's
children) on the command buffer (`put_u32`/`put_str`, Core.roc:4474–4482,
called through `emit_op`). The appends take the copying path, so the
command buffer is not uniquely referenced when they run: each one that has
to grow copies the whole buffer, which is megabytes at 10,000 cells. Two
guesses at the extra reference (the `describe` closures capturing the
block, and the `var` loops threading `Tx`) were tried in a scratch copy of
the engine and changed nothing; the fix belongs in roc-signals (or the Roc
compiler's ownership inference), and the engine here is a verbatim copy.
This is the cliff for opening the window: 10,000 cells take 10–15 s, and
100,000 had not opened after 10 minutes.

The engine's turn was linear in the grid (fixed):: After the layout fix a
turn cost 0.05 ms at 100 cells, 0.15 ms at 1,024 and 1.4–1.9 ms at 10,000,
though it writes one cell's local state and changes one attribute: each
call of the root site copied its 10,000-child list (a 2.4 MB `memcpy` from
`fire_kid`'s `List.replace`, with an incref of every child), because an
erased call borrowed the site's captures. Engine patch 0003 calls sites so
they own them (`patches/roc-signals`, `patches/roc-compiler`
"erased-captures"). Now a hover move's engine time is about 0.1 ms (p50)
at every size measured, 100 to 14,400 cells, and a timer turn under
0.05 ms:

AppKit's own cost (the next wall):: The mount's layout (NSStackView and
Auto Layout solving each cell's fixed width and height and the stacks'
arrangement constraints) is 0.1 s at 1,000 views and 3.2–3.7 s at 10,000,
superlinear, and its memory grows faster still: at 22,500 views the first
`layoutSubtreeIfNeeded` (`updateConstraintsIfNeeded`, `NSBitSetFindNext`,
`__bzero` in CoreAutoLayout) took RSS from 0.5 to 3.4 GB before the process
was killed. That, not the engine, is what stopped N=150. Even with the
engine fixed, a million stack-arranged NSViews is not a reasonable target.
Drawing is not a problem: one cell's repaint is under 0.1 ms.

== What would move the ceiling, by payoff

. *Fix the engine mount's copying* (roc-signals): turns 10,000 cells from
  10–15 s into what the linear part costs (about 0.3 s, extrapolating from
  1,024 cells), and is the precondition for anything past 10,000. Payoff:
  very large; cost: find the shared reference in `mount_elem`/`emit_op`.
. *Make the engine's turn independent of the grid*: done by engine patch
  0003; a turn costs about 0.1 ms at any size measured.
. *A canvas element* (`Ui.canvas` or `Ui.grid`: one view drawing N×N cells
  from a list signal, one CALayer or one `draw(_:)`, hit-testing by
  arithmetic): no NSView, constraint or engine element per cell, so a
  million cells is a few MB and a repaint of one cell's rect. Payoff: the
  only way to a million cells; cost: a new element and a cell-addressed
  wire shape (a row of a table per cell).
. *Fixed frames for fixed-size children* (host): a stack whose children all
  have fixed sizes could place them by frame, without NSStackView's
  constraints, removing most of the 3.2 s of mount layout at 10,000 and the
  gigabytes of solver memory that killed 22,500.
  Payoff: large for mount and memory (it is the wall right after the
  engine's); cost: a second layout path in the host.
. *Virtualise* (as `Ui.list` does for rows): build only the cells in sight.
  Payoff: large for a scrolled grid, none for one that fits the window.
